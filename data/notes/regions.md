# data/regions.json

Campaign shell (M5, five regions since R26): an ordered list of region strips. Nodes unlock in order WITHIN their region; a region itself opens when its `requires` block is satisfied ({ nodes: [...] held, threatGen: N, notoriety: N } — all optional, all ANDed), so the ladder is data and adding a sixth region needs no engine edit. Each node is an encounter from enemies.json; winning holds it, granting income per real-world day (timestamp-computed) and notoriety. threatGens is the Threat Generation ladder — [{gen, at, name, announce}] — replacing the single threatGen2At threshold, which is still read as a fallback so a pre-R26 save sees the world it left. Crossing a rung announces it and unlocks nodes gated with 'threatGen': N. rescueEncounter = the themed battle used for every rescue raid. unlocksFauna: species ids added to the Mail-Order catalog when the node is held. `answer` names the class that beats the region's dominant enemy class ('mixed' where none does) and `demand` says it in the player's language on the map; smoke checks `answer` against the actual rosters AND checks that fauna of that class is already purchasable when the region opens, so the ladder can never ask for anatomy the catalog cannot sell. Region contestation (§3.9 'coalition counter-offensives can contest held regions'): once the threat generation named in `contestation.startsAtGen` is reached, the coalition periodically comes BACK for a node you already hold — anywhere on the map, not just the first region. The schedule is a timestamp (nextContestAt), never a per-tick dice roll, so how often you open the app cannot change how often you are attacked; and the defence window starts the moment you SEE the contest, so being away never costs you a node you were never given the chance to defend. R64: being away is not a month of full pay either. The schedule is replayed through the gap: each convoy arrives when it was due, waits its windowHours at the gate, and — nobody home — leaves without taking anything, the node having paid nothing while it sat there; the next rolls a cooldown after it left. Only a convoy still inside its window when you return is waiting, with its full window from then (news.missed is the one-line summary of the ones that came and went). Adding a region must never require engine edits, and neither does tuning this. `benchGrade` is the part grade a player plausibly holds when the region opens, and `benchTeam` (audit A8) is the number of chimeras the node is tuned for; the balance harness scores each node at ITS OWN grade and team so no cell is measured saturated at one end or floored at the other. Both are per-node overridable, because a strip is not reached all at once: guard_post sits behind Threat Gen 2 and is 4% at the strip's `standard` grade but 48% at `prime`, which is what a player actually brings to it. benchTeam is the answer to "the team size a player has when they reach it" — without it that phrase is undecidable, and A8's gate would just be asserting whatever the balance happened to be. Measured, not wished: it is the smallest team at which the best of the harness archetypes clears the node with room to spare. Income (A9): every node's incomePerDay was raised by half — the wait to afford the Surgery Theater's Tier II gantry ran about eight days of held territory, which is a long time to look at a locked chassis. `completionBonus` is paid per day for holding EVERY node of a region with none of them contested; it is worth roughly the strip's best single node, so finishing a region reads as earning a sixth location. It is suspended the moment a counter-offensive contests any node in the strip, which is what makes defending the cheapest node in a completed region worth as much as defending the richest.

## contestation

startsAtGen/minHeld gate the whole system so early play is untouched. cooldownHours is the gap between counter-offensives, lengthened by cooldownPerDefenceHours for every successful defence of that node — they learn. windowHours is how long you have once you see it. The defence reuses the node's OWN encounter at `escalation` above its authored strength, growing by escalationPerDefence every time you hold the place and capped at escalationMax (R63: the honest 180-day walk saw a node defended twenty-two times at 330% of its garrison; they bring at most double, so a roster that can hold double holds for good): a continuous dial rather than a tier step, because the authored tier ladder is deliberately lumpy (a tier is a band of CONTENT) and +1 tier on an already-top-tier boss is a wall while +1 on a mid encounter is a shrug. A new region therefore costs zero new encounter data, and the ramp is endless and self-paced. rewardScale multiplies the node encounter's reward; holding the line also impounds one of the attacking units, because a conquest reward has to expand what you can CREATE (Law 2) and enemy tech is how this one does. Measured against the balance harness's yardstick team of three at prismatic: a first defence runs 100/98/88/12/42% across the five Greenfield nodes against 100/100/93/30/55% for the original assault.

## expeditions

R179 — THE FIRST THING IN THIS GAME MONEY CANNOT BUY. Every one of the 41 species that shipped before this carried a `mailOrderPrice`, so nothing alive was more than a cash problem and the only scarcity was the balance. An expedition is the other supply line: crew go into a region for real-world hours, the roll is seeded at launch like every other sealed outcome here, and what comes back is whatever that region's own table hands over.

WHAT THE PRICE IS, and it is deliberately not money. The crew are unavailable for jobs and fights for the whole trip — five readers enforce it (`jobSlots` and `freeCrew` in campaign/operations.js, `canBringMore`, `fitTeam` and `suggestTeam` in campaign/warroom.js) and they read ONE predicate so they cannot drift apart. `fundsPerCrewHour` is 9 against a board that pays about 35 a crew-hour at roughly half odds, so an expedition is a deliberate loss on cash: the animal is the reason to go, and a player who wants money works the board instead. That is the trade R116 made scarce and this spends.

`rarityFloor` IS THE DECISION. Anything above `common` needs a trip of at least that many hours with at least that many crew, so a short one-crew outing to the Drowned Quarter physically cannot roll the Manta however many times it is run. Without the floor, "which region, how long, with whom" collapses into "tap the cheapest one repeatedly", which is the jobs-board defect R116 spent a whole milestone undoing at the other end of the county. With it, the long trip is a thing you save up crew for.

ONE PARTY AT A TIME, for the reason the standing arrangement is one at a time: a list of switches to turn all on is not a choice. `expeditionReadyAt` is the van unpacking afterwards and is the same shape as `sparRefillAt` and `boardRefillAt` — a time in the past means ready now, so a save that has never seen this field behaves correctly the moment it is migrated.

## regions[].expedition

`finds` is a weighted table and it is the whole content surface: adding a species to a region is a JSON edit, and R127 made `tools/gen-parts.js` the authority for the parts that come with it, so a new animal reaches the Dex without an engine edit anywhere. `tools/reach.js` reads these tables directly rather than keeping a list of its own — the rule R95 set is that a table of ids goes stale the day somebody adds the next animal.

THE TABLES ARE THEMATIC RATHER THAN BALANCED, on purpose. Greenfield is hedgerows and one municipal pond; Kestrel Reach is thermals and nesting sites; the Drowned Quarter is flooded streets and the one thing under the pier. A region a player has not opened offers nothing, because `expeditionRegions` runs every candidate through the campaign's own `regionOpen` — an expedition can never reach past the map, so the Manta is a mid-game animal without anybody writing a level gate for it.

`brief` is the sentence on the launcher. What a longer or fuller trip would ALSO reach is derived from this table by `findsBeyond` and never authored, so the screen advertises a species the day it is added here and cannot advertise one that has been taken out.

ONLY `uncommon` HAS A FLOOR TODAY, and that is deliberate rather than unfinished. `rare` and `unique` are queued (ROADMAP R179) and nothing carries either tier yet; a floor of 36 or 48 hours against `hourOptions` of 4/12/24 would be a rule that no trip this game offers can satisfy, which is the dead-content failure `tools/reach.js` exists to prevent, one level up. The smoke gate asserts that every declared floor is reachable by SOME legal trip, so the session that ships a rare has to ship the hours to go and get it in the same diff.
