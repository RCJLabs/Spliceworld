# data/facility.json

Facility upgrades (ROADMAP §3.10): menu purchases that expand what you can CREATE, never what you can grind (Law 2 — every conquest reward must expand creation). A track is an ordered list of levels; you buy them in order, each gated by money and usually by territory. `grants` is read by the systems that care: the Surgery Theater reads frames and sockets, the Containment bay reads `rehab` and its scales, so unlocking the Rumbler chassis, the second organ bay, or the Reorientation Wing is a data edit, not an engine one. Levels beyond the last are simply absent — adding Tier III means appending an object here. Other tracks named in §3.10 (Gene Scanner, Extractor efficiency, Infirmary speed, Containment Cannon mk2) drop into the same shape. R87 — a top tier on the four tracks that still had a knob to turn, priced against the economy the endgame actually reaches: measured over six 180-day walks, every track was maxed by median day 28 (BEFORE the county falls on day 35) and funds then ran to a median $864k at +$5,128/day with nothing left to buy. These four cost $480k together, which is most of a campaign's bank. Theater and Scanner deliberately have NO tier 4: Theater's next step is an eighth socket and Scanner's two grants are both already true, so both need an engine change rather than a data one, and a level that grants nothing would be a price with no purchase behind it.

## tracks[].tuning

Rehabilitation constants (§3.6: salvage it OR rehabilitate it into your roster). Hours and fee are read off the specimen so a rival's apex monster is a longer, dearer programme than a scrappy one; `hourScale`/`feeScale` on a level make the whole track cheaper as you invest in it. The two knobs are deliberately orthogonal: the CLOCK releases the specimen, the SESSIONS decide who walks out. sessionGapFraction is a share of the programme's own length rather than a fixed cooldown, so the full curriculum always fits inside the programme — otherwise the scrappiest specimens, whose programmes are shortest, would be the hardest ones to befriend.

## upkeep

Daily upkeep. Stock animals pay their species' upkeepPerDay (unchanged). A chimera pays for the chassis it rides, the grade of every part bolted to it, and the power those parts draw — all three read off data the genome already carries, so a new part, grade or frame is priced automatically. Instability is a surcharge because a creature trying to come apart needs watching. The spread across grades is deliberately steep (1 -> 22 per part) and the flat terms deliberately small: a first standard chimera costs about $21/day against a $40 stipend, and building it also takes an animal OUT of the herd, so building one can never bankrupt a player who has not conquered anything yet (the R11 floor), while a stable of prismatic monsters runs past $150/day each and genuinely requires territory. Net income (territory + stipend - upkeep) is what the player actually banks, and every screen that shows money shows it, because a treadmill you cannot see is just a number that will not go up.
