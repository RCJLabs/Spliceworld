# data/scars.json

Injury scarring (ROADMAP §3.5: 'untreated injuries can scar into permanent trait tradeoffs'). A battle injury opens an Infirmary timer; pay to treat it and it clears clean, ignore it and there is a chance it sets badly and stays. EVERY SCAR IS TWO-SIDED, which is the whole design: a scar is character, not a punishment, so missing a treatment window is interesting rather than ruinous — and some scars are net GOOD for a given build, which makes 'leave it and see' a real choice rather than a mistake. `vs` narrows an effect to opponents carrying one of those tags, which is what makes the roadmap's own example ('Chompers now fears jeeps: +Evasion vs. vehicles, −Accuracy vs. vehicles') expressible as data. Effects: acc is percentage points on the move roll; evasion is a fraction off the attacker's chance to connect; power and armor are fractions of damage dealt and taken; speed and regen are flat additions to the stat. Adding a scar is one object here and nothing else.

`tuning.injuries` (R193) is every clock the Infirmary sees, in one table: the battle's, a last stand's, a rescue's whiplash and a failed job's bruise. Each states its `hours` (a roll across the range), whether a better Infirmary shortens it (`tierScaled`: a rescue's whiplash is not, the other three are) and the `names` a player sees on the card. They used to be typed in four modules, so no policy could read the clock a patient brings in, and the walker's vet bill priced every refusal on the battle's alone. The code that inflicts each one reads its row (`injuryClock` in battle/statblock.js), and smoke moves each row and watches the engine follow. A new kind is a new row here, a share in `WALK_INJURY_MIX` in tools/sim.js, and an entry on smoke's clock roll.

A HOLD IS NOT A WOUND (R193). A failed espionage job is "a night in a holding pen and a stern letter" (R180), so it rides the same clock (the creature is away, and everything that asks whether a creature is free still asks `isInjured`) with `reason: "detained"`, and nothing else: no vet shortens it or bills for it, the Infirmary will not sell it ("does not do bail"), it cannot set badly, and the Pens say where the creature is. The test is `isHold` in splice/facility.js. Measured over sixteen 180-day campaigns before the rule, holds were 8.5% of injuries but 41% of injury hours, 94% of the hours the vets turned away, $32,800 of Nurse Gauze's $75,533 bill, 46 Infirmary buy-outs and 105 of 423 scars. A hold's hours live with its mission (`detainHours` in data/missions.json), because a hold is not a clock this table's reader treats.
