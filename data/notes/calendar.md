# data/calendar.json

The county calendar (ROADMAP §9, R105). WHY THIS EXISTS: the game ran entirely on real timestamps — CLAUDE.md's own rule — and nothing in it knew what time it was. Verified before a line was written: every hit for "season", "weather" or "time of day" across the whole tree was flavour prose (a dossier saying two donors "disagree about weather on a cellular level"; a news line about salvage crews working until dawn). Zero mechanics, five static themes, and the Ranch at 3 a.m. identical to the Ranch at 3 p.m.

NOTHING HERE IS WRITTEN TO THE SAVE, and that is the design rather than an economy. `seasonOf`, `weatherOf` and `skyOf` are all `(save, content, now) -> value` over `createdAt` and `seed`, both of which already existed, so R105 needs no `SAVE_VERSION` bump and a month that passed while the tab was closed simply passed. It is CLAUDE.md's "timers are timestamps, not intervals" applied to the calendar itself.

HUSBANDRY, NEVER POWER. A season changes how fast condition drifts (`decayScale`), how long an egg takes (`incubationScale`) and how likely a variant is in the breeding window (`variantBonus`). It may never touch a number a fight reads, and `tools/smoke.js` enforces that by scanning every key in `seasons` for anything whose name touches a stat — so the next person cannot add `powerScale` here because it was easy. The scales are also bounded to 0.5–1.5 by the same gate: a season is weather, not a wall.

THE SEASONAL SHELF WAS BUILT AND CUT, and the measurement is why. The R105 entry said a season should hang the Travelling Menagerie R95 wanted. R95 had already measured that idea away — "there is nothing to rotate", because by day 180 the walk holds 22 or 23 of 23 nodes on a median $249,000, which already opens 33 of 41 species. Built anyway and isolated across five seeds, ANY shelf broke R142's splice floor: none 28/29/28/30/30, one species 24/40/28/30/30, three species 22/30/23/30/22. The cause is the PRICE rather than the count — R95 taught the catalogue to advertise anatomy you have never held and the walker collects, so a season parading species past a player redirects the money that would have become chimeras. A rotation that costs a shipped milestone its floor is not a feature, so `stocks` is not in the data and `seasonOf` no longer reports one.

WEATHER CARRIES NO MECHANICAL EFFECT, and three attempts is how that was settled. The entry proposed "rain slows the ring's refill". Scaling the READ made a wet ring refill FASTER, because `sparCharges` divides the outstanding wait by the regen. Scaling the SPEND made one charge read as two and R43's own gate caught it. Scaling both ate a charge stamped under yesterday's weather, because every candidate hook here is a timestamp-derived bucket and a multiplier that changes daily cannot be applied to one without occasionally charging the player for a day that has already gone. So weather is a line and a sky: `ringRegenScale` is not in the data and nothing reads it.

THE SKY IS DATA AND IT IS LAZY. `sky.bands` are hour bands with a `tint` and an `alpha`; `ui/sky.js` turns them into four SVG shapes and is loaded AFTER the first paint. That split is arithmetic rather than taste: R105 started from 48 eager modules of 49, 311.2 KB of eager code of 314 and 1,029 KB of first paint of 1,034. `campaign/calendar.js` is eager because the husbandry numbers it scales are read inside `tickWorld`, which boot runs on the first frame; a sky is chrome and `tools/boot.js` exists to keep geometry out of the player's way. The tint is an overlay INSIDE the header and never redefines a theme token, or dusk would make Blueprint at 7 p.m. a sixth theme nobody designed.

ADDING A SEASON OR A WEATHER IS A JSON EDIT. `order` drives the cycle and `seasonDays` its length; an id in `order` with no entry in `seasons` simply scales nothing rather than throwing, because a missing tuning is a content gap and every default here is the identity (1, 1, 0, []).

TWO NUMBERS HERE WERE TUNED BY MEASUREMENT, NOT BY TASTE, and both were wrong first.

THE YEAR NETS TO 1.0 ON EVERY HUSBANDRY SCALE, and `tools/smoke.js` enforces it. The first authored set ran decay 0.9/1.2/1.0/0.75 and incubation 0.85/0.95/1.05/1.2 — means of 0.96 and 1.01, which looked harmless and was not. Across R142's own five seeds it took the Theater from 35/32/28/30/33 splices to 25/29/20/30/27: a fifth of the output, and one seed straight through the floor R142 spent a milestone establishing. A quarter of the year at 1.2 is not paid back by three quarters at 0.95, so the invariant has to be about the YEAR rather than about any one season. A calendar redistributes; it does not tax.

AND THE SHELF IS THE CHEAP END OF THE CATALOGUE, for a reason nobody would guess. Isolated across five seeds: the husbandry scales alone cost the Theater essentially nothing (28/29/28/30/30 against a neutral 35/32/28/30/33), while the seasonal shelf ALONE took seed 99 to 22 and the two together to 20. It is not how many species a season carries — one expensive species does the same damage as three — it is the PRICE. R95 taught the catalogue to advertise anatomy you have never held, and the walker collects: a season parading scorpions ($210) and owls ($175) past a player redirects the money that would have become chimeras. The shipped shelf is drawn from species at $140 or less, which reads 28/29/28/30/30 — the scales' own number, with the rotation free. Thirteen cheap species are conquest-gated, so it still genuinely adds stock.

Adding an expensive species to `stocks` is therefore a balance change, not a content edit, and the splice floor in tools/smoke.js is what will say so.
